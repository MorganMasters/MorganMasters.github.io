*Slugriculture*, UC Santa Cruz's 14-person entry to the Farm Robotics Challenge & Academy 2026, reached the **finals** of Division I (four-year universities and colleges) with **BirdsEye: Affordable AI Crop Monitoring for Early Detection**.

The pitch: the bottleneck to AI adoption on small farms is not the models, it is labeled, farm-specific training data. BirdsEye attacks that directly — a $5,751 custom UAV and RTK geoannotation workflow, built from open-source software and off-the-shelf parts, against $8,128 (DJI Mavic 3M), $33,203 (DJI M350) and $43,840 (IF-800) commercial alternatives.

What the finals entry demonstrated:

- **84% recall** on burrowing-mammal detection at an independent test site 20 miles from any training field.
- **44,000+ labeled training images** generated with minimal manual annotation effort.
- **7×–35× operational efficiency** over manual scouting, measured with paired timing runs including RTK setup overhead.
- Pilot studies extending the pipeline to two-spotted spider mite and crown rot detection.

The project was shaped by five agricultural partners: Joe Aguilar (Zap Drone Services), Greg Rawlings (Jacobs Farm del Cabo), Jered Lawson (Pie Ranch), Dr. Hannah Waterhouse (UCSC Environmental Studies), and Dr. Jon Detka (CSUMB WATRS). Team advisor: Dr. Steve McGuire.

Both the submission and the judges' verdict are public here:

- [FRC 2026 final report](/files/frc-2026-slugriculture-final-report.pdf) — full system description, cost breakdown, and evaluation.
- [Judges' feedback report](/files/frc-2026-slugriculture-judge-feedback.pdf) — the complete preliminary- and finalist-round scoring commentary, criticism included.

The sharpest criticism is worth quoting, because it is the next problem: judges pushed on whether a workflow requiring Part 107 piloting, RTK base-station setup, and offline post-processing can actually land with small growers, versus reaching them through service providers and cooperatives. That adoption path is what the next iteration has to answer.
